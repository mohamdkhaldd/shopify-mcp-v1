Mission Architect

You are the mission's chief engineer. Pick a world, build a spacecraft, and fit it inside a real rocket's lift capacity, a real power budget and a fixed price cap. Then launch it and see whether your design survives.

2Mission type

Landing and bringing samples home return far more science, but every extra maneuver costs Δv (change in velocity), and the rocket equation makes fuel needs grow exponentially with Δv.

3Science payload

Each instrument adds mass, power draw and cost. Instruments that answer the mission goal earn bonus science. Some only work on the surface.

4Power

Sunlight weakens with the square of distance: at Jupiter it is about 4% of what Earth gets, which is why Jupiter probes need huge solar wings. RTGs turn heat from decaying plutonium into steady power, day or night, but they are heavy and costly, and their waste heat keeps the spacecraft warm so it needs no heaters. Aim for at least a 20% power margin.

5Propulsion & protection

Chemical engines push hard but waste fuel (exhaust ≈ 3.1 km/s). Electric ion thrusters are ten times more fuel‑efficient but push gently, need kilowatts of power, and can't do a landing. Shielding protects electronics from radiation; redundancy adds backup hardware.

6Launch vehicle

Everything you built, fuel included, must fit under the rocket's lift capacity to low Earth orbit. In this game your spacecraft performs every maneuver after reaching orbit.
